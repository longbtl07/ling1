EXAM ESSENTIALS
1. Morpheme Braces & Notation:

In Morphemic Analysis, write lexical roots in ALL CAPS in braces: {WRITE}. Countable singular nouns MANDATORILY require {sg}; present/base verbs require {pres}.

2. Strictly 8 Inflectional Affixes:

Never invent extra inflectional affixes. English possesses only 8 suffixes. All others are derivational or bound roots!

3. Consonant & Vowel Formulas:

Consonants: [Voicing] + [Place] + [Manner] (e.g. /b/ = voiced bilabial stop). Monophthongs: [Length] + [Height] + [Backness] + [Rounding] (e.g. /uː/ = long high back rounded vowel).

4. Zero-Trap Minimal Pairs:

Must have the exact same number of phonemes in identical phonetic environment, differing by exactly ONE sound (e.g. bet - vet, NOT best - vest).

📖 Theoretical Definitions (Định Nghĩa Chuẩn Học Thuật)

UPPERCASE CLASSIFICATION
1. MORPHEME (Hình thái tố / Hình vị)

The smallest meaningful linguistic unit or grammatical unit. It is an indivisible linguistic sign consisting of phonological form and semantic/grammatical meaning.

Đơn vị nhỏ nhất của ngôn ngữ mang nghĩa hoặc chức năng ngữ pháp; không thể chia nhỏ hơn thành các đơn vị mang nghĩa.

2. GRAMMATICAL MORPHEME (Hình vị ngữ pháp)

Expresses grammatical relationships, syntactic functions, or closed-class structural meanings.

  • FUNCTION WORD (Free Grammatical Morpheme): Free morphemes that serve grammatical roles: Prepositions (in, on, at), Pronouns (he, she, they), Auxiliaries (can, will, be, have), Conjunctions (and, but, although), Determiners (the, a, this).
  • INFLECTIONAL AFFIX (Bound Grammatical Morpheme): Purely grammatical markers. In English, there are STRICTLY 8 INFLECTIONAL AFFIXES (all suffixes):
    Inflectional AffixGrammatical FunctionExamples
    -sNoun plural (số nhiều)book-s, cat-s, teach-er-s
    -'sNoun possessive (sở hữu cách)Mary-'s, boy-'s
    -sVerb 3rd person singular present (ngôi 3 số ít)work-s, run-s, teach-es
    -edVerb simple past (thì quá khứ đơn)walk-ed, visit-ed
    -en / -edVerb past participle (quá khứ phân từ)eat-en, driv-en, walk-ed
    -ingVerb present participle / gerund (phân từ hiện tại / danh động từ)play-ing, walk-ing
    -erAdjective / Adverb comparative (so sánh hơn)fast-er, tall-er
    -estAdjective / Adverb superlative (so sánh nhất)fast-est, tall-est
3. LEXICAL MORPHEME (Hình vị từ vựng)

Expresses dictionary/lexical meaning; belongs to open-class categories.

  • CONTENT WORD (Free Lexical Morpheme): Can stand alone as independent vocabulary items: Nouns (book, student), Verbs (run, teach), Adjectives (happy, bright), Adverbs (quickly, clockwise).
  • DERIVATIONAL AFFIX (Bound Lexical Morpheme): Prefixes (un-, re-, mis-, pre-) and Suffixes (-ness, -ize, -ful, -ation, -ly). Can change part of speech (e.g. rich [Adj] ➔ enrich [V]) or significantly change meaning (e.g. align ➔ misalign).
4. FREE MORPHEME vs. BOUND MORPHEME
  • FREE MORPHEME: Can stand alone as an independent word in sentences (e.g., open, tour, book, gentle).
  • BOUND MORPHEME: Cannot stand alone; must attach to another form (e.g., re-, -ist, -ed, -ceive).

⚠️ Mandatory Rules & Usage Conditions (Quy Tắc Bắt Buộc & Bẫy Thi)

EXAM ALERT
Rule 1: Attachment Position (Vị trí gắn kết):

Derivational affixes always attach closer to the root than Inflectional affixes (e.g., teach-er-s [Root + Derivational -er + Inflectional -s], NEVER *teach-s-er).

Rule 2: Word Class Integrity (Bảo toàn từ loại):

Inflectional affixes NEVER change the part of speech of the word (e.g., book [N] ➔ books [N]; walk [V] ➔ walked [V]). Only derivational affixes can change word classes.

Rule 3: Stacking Limits (Giới hạn xếp chồng):

A word can have multiple derivational affixes (e.g., un-happi-ness, de-activ-at-ion), but only one inflectional affix per word class category at the very end.

🔍 Niche & Special Cases (Các Trường Hợp Đặc Biệt Phải Nhớ)

HIGH-DISTINCTION
1. BOUND ROOT (Căn tố ràng buộc)

Roots that carry primary lexical meaning across a word family but cannot occur in isolation:

• -ceive (con-ceive, re-ceive, de-ceive)
• -struct (con-struct, de-struct, in-struct)
• -vert (con-vert, re-vert, in-vert)
• -mit (trans-mit, per-mit, ad-mit)

⚠️ Bẫy: "Bound morphs are called affixes" ➔ FALSE (vì còn có Bound roots!).
2. ENCLITICS (Dạng bám đính)

Bound forms phonologically derived from independent words that attach to preceding words:

• Auxiliaries: 'll (will), 've (have), 're (are), 'm (am), 'd (would/had)
• Negative marker: -n't in won't, can't, isn't

3. ZERO MORPHEME & SUPPLETION

Cases where no overt physical affix segment is physically added:

• Zero morph: sheep (pl) = {SHEEP} + {pl}; hit (past) = {HIT} + {past}
• Suppletion: wrote = {WRITE} + {past}; better = {GOOD} + {compr}; least = {LITTLE} + {supl}

🔍 Comprehensive 8-Process Taxonomy (Toàn Bộ 8 Quy Trình Cấu Tạo Từ)

FULL SYLLABUS
Process · Quy trình English Definition & Mechanism Subtypes & Diagnostic Clues Authentic Examples
1. DERIVATION
Phái sinh		 Adding derivational affixes (prefixes or suffixes) to a base, creating a new lexical item with new meaning or word class. • Prefixes: change meaning (re-, un-, dis-, mis-)
• Suffixes: change word class (-ment, -ness, -ize, -ful, -ly)		 unhappy, rewrite, teacher, national, darkness, clockwise, broaden
2. REDUPLICATION
Láy từ		 Forming a new word by doubling all or part of a base syllable/root. • Exact: papa, mama, so-so, goody-goody
• Ablaut: vowel alternates (flip-flop, zig-zag, ping-pong, chit-chat)
• Rhyme: consonant changes (roly-poly, walkie-talkie, helter-skelter) 		ping-pong, zig-zag, walkie-talkie, flip-flop, bye-bye, hocus-pocus
3. CONVERSION / FUNCTION SHIFT
Chuyển loại		 Assigning an existing word to a new syntactic category (part of speech) with zero physical change in form. • Commonization: Proper noun becomes common word (cashmere, china, chauvinism, sandwich, boycott, jeans)
• Stress Shift: Primary stress shift changes category ('record [N] vs re'cord [V]) 		a run (V➔N), to bottle (N➔V), to microwave (N➔V), a look (V➔N), to dirty (Adj➔V)
4. COMPOUNDING
Ghép từ		 Combining two or more independent free roots to form a single lexical item. • Stress Rule: Leftmost primary stress ('blackboard vs 'black 'board)
• Headness Rule: Rightmost element determines syntactic class (homeland is Noun because land is Noun) 		bookcase, fast food, teapot, daydream, sunflower, skydive, greenhouse
5. BLENDING
Pha trộn (Portmanteau)		 Fusing non-morphemic pieces of two distinct words: usually the beginning of word 1 + the end of word 2. Truncated parts are merged (A... + ...B), not whole words. sm(oke) + (f)og ➔ smog, mo(tor) + (ho)tel ➔ motel, brunch, Eurovision, stagflation, cyborg
6. BACK FORMATION
Phái sinh ngược		 Forming a shorter word of a new class (usually a verb) by deleting an affix-like ending from an existing complex noun. Opposite of derivation. The longer noun existed historically first! emotion ➔ emote, peddler ➔ peddle, babysitter ➔ babysit, editor ➔ edit, television ➔ televise
7. SHORTENING
Rút ngắn từ		 Deleting phonological segments from a word. • Clipping: Dropping syllables while keeping meaning & class intact (fanatic ➔ fan, refrigerator ➔ fridge, gas, lab, exam, flu)
• Acronym: Initial letters pronounced as a word (WASP, NATO, Radar, Laser, AIDS, UNESCO)
• Initialism: Initial letters spelled letter-by-letter (a.m., p.m., B.C., FBI, VIP, CD, BBC, ATM) 		fridge, fan, NATO, radar, FBI, VIP, BBC, flu, gym, laser
8. ROOT CREATION
Tạo gốc từ mới		 Inventing an entirely brand-new root morpheme out of nothing, without using existing words or affixes. • Onomatopoeia: sound imitation (meow, cuckoo, sizzle, buzz, splash)
• Literary Coinage: creative invention (dwindle, chortle, nerd, quark)
• Brand Names: trade marks becoming roots (Xerox, Kodak, Google, Nylon) 		meow, cuckoo, dwindle, Xerox, Kodak, nylon

🎙️ Core Vocal Organs (Các Cơ Quan Cấu Âm Chính)

ANATOMICAL FUNCTIONS
Vocal Organ · Cơ quan English Definition & Function Phonetic Roles & Exam Traps
Larynx
Thanh quản		 A cartilaginous structure situated at the top of the trachea (the "Adam's apple") housing the vocal cords. Houses the vocal folds; primary valve regulating pulmonic airstream.
Vocal Cords / Folds
Dây thanh		 Two muscular elastic bands stretched across the larynx that vibrate to produce voicing. • Voiced: vibrating (/b, d, g, v, z, m, n/ & 100% of vowels)
• Voiceless: held open, no vibration (/p, t, k, f, s, θ, ʃ, tʃ, h/)
Pharynx
Hầu / Họng		 The muscular cavity immediately above the larynx and behind the oral and nasal cavities. Acts as a primary resonance chamber for both oral and nasal airstreams.
Hard Palate
Ngạc cứng		 The bony, rigid anterior part of the roof of the mouth, functioning as a passive articulator. Passive articulator contacted by tongue blade/front: creates palatal sounds (e.g. /j/).
Soft Palate / Velum
Ngạc mềm		 The movable muscular posterior fold of the roof of the mouth that acts as a valve regulating nasal cavity airflow. • Raised against pharynx: seals off nasal cavity ➔ Oral sounds
• Lowered: air escapes via nasal cavity ➔ Nasal sounds (/m, n, ŋ/)

👃 Oral vs. Nasal Sounds

• Oral sounds: Soft palate (velum) is raised, sealing the nasal tract. 100% of airflow escapes solely through the oral cavity (covers most English sounds).
• Nasal sounds: Soft palate is lowered, allowing airflow into the nasal cavity. English has strictly 3 nasal consonants: /m/ (bilabial), /n/ (alveolar), /ŋ/ (velar).

🔊 Voiced vs. Voiceless Sounds

• Voiced: Vocal folds are brought together loosely and vibrate in the airstream (/b, d, g, v, ð, z, ʒ, dʒ, m, n, ŋ, l, r, w, j/ + all vowels).
• Voiceless: Vocal folds are pulled wide apart; air passes freely through glottis without vibration (/p, t, k, f, θ, s, ʃ, tʃ, h/).

⚖️ Phoneme vs. Allophone Contrast

FUNCTIONAL CONTRAST
PHONEME (Âm vị)

• Written in slashes: /p/, /b/, /t/
• Abstract, meaning-distinguishing sound unit in the mental grammar.
• Occurs in contrastive distribution: replacing one with another creates a distinct word with new meaning (/pɪn/ vs /bɪn/).

ALLOPHONE (Biến thể âm vị)

• Written in square brackets: [pʰ], [p], [ʔ]
• Concrete phonetic realization variant of a single phoneme.
• Occurs in complementary distribution or free variation; swapping allophones NEVER changes word meaning.

⚙️ Maximum Onset Principle (MOP) Algorithm

SYLLABIFICATION

MOP Rule: Where two vowels are separated by consonants, assign as many consonants as possible to the ONSET of the following syllable, provided the consonant cluster is a valid word-initial cluster in English phonotactics.

aroma: /ə . ˈrəʊ . mə/
Intervocalic /r/ and /m/ are assigned as onsets of syllable 2 and 3.
algebra: /ˈæl . dʒɪ . brə/
/br/ is a permitted English onset, so both consonants attach to syllable 3.
athlete: /ˈæθ . liːt/
/θl/ is NOT a valid English onset; /θ/ remains in coda 1, /l/ becomes onset 2.
